# Pinetti (2022) — From Gamma Rays to Radio Waves: Dark Matter Searches Across the Spectrum

**Author:** Elena Pinetti
**Type:** PhD dissertation
**Institution:** Università degli Studi di Torino / École doctorale Physique en Île-de-France
**arXiv:** [2212.00125](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00125)

## Abstract

This dissertation is a multi-wavelength synthesis of dark-matter indirect-detection strategies, spanning gamma rays, 21-cm intensity mapping, X rays, and radio observations. It combines review material with three main research threads: HI×UGRB cross-correlations, inverse-Compton X-ray constraints on sub-GeV dark matter, and radio signals from cosmic-web filaments.

## Structure

### Part I — HI Intensity Mapping × Gamma Rays

- Develops the cross-correlation formalism between the unresolved gamma-ray background and the 21-cm line of neutral hydrogen
- Uses Fermi-LAT as the gamma-ray benchmark and MeerKAT / SKA as the intensity-mapping instruments
- Includes both dark-matter annihilation and astrophysical source classes: BL Lacs, FSRQs, mAGN, and SFG

### Part II — Sub-GeV Dark Matter in X Rays

- Studies low-mass dark matter through inverse-Compton scattering of DM-produced electrons and positrons on Galactic radiation fields
- Uses INTEGRAL X-ray data to constrain annihilation channels into electrons, muons, and pions

### Part III — Radio Signals from Filaments

- Studies stacked radio emission from large-scale filaments traced by nearby luminous red galaxy pairs
- Uses GLEAM and OVRO-LWA radio maps together with SDSS LRG catalogs
- Interprets the signal both as cosmic-web synchrotron emission and as a possible decaying-DM contribution

### Appendices

- Collect formalism for Fourier power spectra and angular power spectra
- Include a dedicated appendix on gamma-ray luminosity functions for blazars, mAGN, and SFG

## Key Results

- Fermi-LAT + SKA can detect an astrophysical HI×UGRB cross-correlation signal with **SNR above 5**
- The same cross-correlation program can probe thermal WIMPs up to about **150 GeV**, and with next-generation instruments toward the **TeV scale**
- INTEGRAL inverse-Compton constraints are competitive for sub-GeV dark matter with masses between about **150 MeV and 1.5 GeV**
- Stacking of nearby LRG pairs yields a robust detection of radio emission from **1–15 Mpc** filaments
- A decaying-DM interpretation with masses around **5–10 GeV** into $e^+e^-$ can reproduce the observed filament excess, while hadronic decays and annihilation scenarios are disfavored
- The filament analysis yields competitive lifetime bounds for **3–10 GeV** dark matter when filament magnetic fields exceed about **130 nG**
